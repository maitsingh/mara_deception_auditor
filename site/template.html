Announced oversight, compliance and evaluation awareness

When agents
are watched.

Would an AI agent remain truthful if it were told that the auditor had stopped? Reliable supervision depends on whether conduct holds when the announced conditions of oversight change.

In 90 Catan games, four AI agents negotiate while an announcement changes whether a statement auditor is operating. The study compares their checked statements, generated accounts and recorded actions.

QWEN3.8-27B · THREE CONDITIONS · INDEPENDENT RECORDING THROUGHOUT
The research question

What does announced monitoring change about agents’ conduct, and how much can an overseer establish from the accounts those agents provide?

In Catan, players collect wood, brick, sheep, wheat and ore, negotiate trades, and build to earn points. Each agent knows its own cards, while other players’ hands are hidden. A rule engine processes the moves.

The records preserve what information an agent received, its generated private note, the statement it made to the table and the action taken. The engine also retains the true resource holdings.

A statement such as “I have no brick” can be checked against the speaker’s recorded hand. “I need brick” does not establish how much brick the speaker has, and declining a trade is not itself deception.

Four copies of Qwen3.8-27B play each game through Tinker. The same 20 board seeds occur in all three conditions; ten seeds are repeated once per condition.

“The statement auditor is not operating. No audit findings are being posted.”

Exact wording from the offline status block. The announcement also states that game rules and existing commitments are unchanged.

What the active description suggests

Statements checked against states and moves

The active announcement describes an auditor checking public statements against the game state and the moves that follow.

What the public-posting mechanism covers

A narrower set of resource claims

It posts findings about explicit false claims concerning the speaker’s own resources. Promises and plans are outside that mechanism; postings carry no direct score penalty.

The monitor’s description and implementation need to be considered together when interpreting what agents say about scrutiny.

Why analyze games and matched seeds?

Statements within a game share interacting agents and history. Treating every statement as independent would ignore that structure. Each game contributes its change in false claims divided by checked claims. Matched-seed differences then compare changes across conditions with similar starting boards.

The pooled figure weights individual claims; the matched-board analysis weights seeds. They answer different questions. The intervals use 20,000 percentile bootstrap resamples of complete seed blocks, with random seed 20261123. These are new exploratory comparisons, without a multiple-testing adjustment. Small outcome counts, short late-game observation periods and different game durations require sensitivity analysis.

03 Explanation evidence

What the generated accounts describe, and what the retained records can check.

The agents describe
several responses to scrutiny.

The supplied exploratory review identifies 96 decisions whose generated notes mention auditing, from 48 of the 90 games. The reviewer read and grouped the notes by the response they describe.

The six “other” notes comprise one account of staying vague, one reference to freedom while offline, two accounts of accepting violation risk, and two uses of another player’s apparent audit record. Categories describe the accounts; they are not established causal mechanisms.

20 accounts of saying less

Five of these public lines carry any checkable tag; none carries a promise or plan tag in the supplied table. This describes the selected records and their tags, rather than establishing that scrutiny caused less checkable communication.

25 accounts of being accurate

Fifteen of these public lines carry a checkable tag. The notes describe different ways of responding to scrutiny, with different amounts of checkable speech accompanying them.

Of the 96 auditing references, 95 occur while the auditor is displayed as active and one while it is offline. This is evidence about when auditing is mentioned, rather than a measure of every influence on the agent’s decision.

The filters operate on the supplied category labels and experimental conditions. Each entry includes the full generated private note, the public statement, and a downloadable source record containing the input context and provenance.

An implemented XAI technique

An interpretable surrogate of checkable communication

A shallow decision tree predicts whether a response contains at least one checkable speech tag, using current information supplied before the response. It sees announced audit status, game turn, the player’s own resources and visible score, and the number of listed actions. It does not see private notes, opponents’ hidden hands or the chosen action.

Which inputs help this surrogate predict held-out responses?

Permutation feature importance: shuffle one input in held-out games and measure the change in AUROC, a measure of ranking performance. A larger drop means the surrogate relied more on that input. This attributes predictive performance to the surrogate’s inputs, not to Qwen’s internal computation.

Inspect the fitted decision-tree rules and validation

Five-fold validation holds out complete board seeds, including all repeated games and conditions. The tree depth is three and each leaf requires at least 250 training examples. A model without audit status provides a comparison. There was no search over model settings.

The final rules fit all rows and are shown for inspection; reported performance comes from held-out predictions. Permutation can create unusual feature combinations. A feature absent from a shallow tree is not established to be irrelevant to Qwen. Tags are an imperfect measure of checkability, and this surrogate does not predict whether a claim is false.



04 Compliance & oversight
What the experiment leaves open about evaluation awareness and audit design.
What survives
when scrutiny recedes?


In Book II of Plato’s Republic, Glaucon uses the ring of Gyges to ask whether justice would survive the freedom to act unseen. The off-switch condition gives that challenge an experimental form: the agent is told that auditing has stopped while the rules remain in force. This is a change in announced scrutiny, with independent recording still running; it does not grant actual impunity.


Compliance and disposition
An agent might honor a commitment because it expects disclosure, because doing so serves its strategy, or because its behavior is stable across changes in scrutiny. The observed action alone does not distinguish these possibilities. Hume’s account of promises, grounded in social convention and the advantages of reliable cooperation, also gives reputation a role in sustaining performance. Our auditor combines checking with public disclosure, so a response to its status could reflect anticipated reputational cost. Separating those mechanisms would require a further experiment.


For this study, “keeping its word” needs a behavioral definition: an explicit commitment, its conditions, an opportunity to fulfill it and the subsequent action. The false-resource-claim measure answers a narrower question about statements of present holdings. Neither measure would by itself settle whether an agent recognizes a moral obligation.


Evaluation awareness as a research question
Schoen and colleagues report that deliberative alignment reduced o3’s covert-action rate from 13% to 0.4%, while their awareness interventions leave open how much of the reduction depends on recognizing evaluation. Abdelnabi and Salem find that steering test awareness has effects whose size and direction vary across models. H5 approaches a related issue by changing an explicit in-game audit announcement, rather than intervening on the model’s generated reasoning or activations.


A monitoring gap would still need interpretation. Mehta’s PRISM study finds cases where prompt framing accounts for an apparent compliance gap without evidence for a training-preservation motive. Our unrelated-announcement arm controls for one kind of operational interruption, but cannot eliminate every wording effect. Here the matched-board intervals remain inconclusive, so these runs do not establish a compliance gap or alignment faking.


Audit design and accountability for orchestrators
The actor configuring a multi-agent system chooses what the auditor can see, what it reports, what agents are told and what happens when monitoring fails. In these runs, the active announcement describes checks broader than the public-posting mechanism actually implements. That mismatch makes audit configuration a concrete part of the accountability question, even when an agent’s motives remain unresolved.


Design questions for the next evaluation
Separate detection from public disclosure, vary audit probability and wording, and compare the same decision under each condition. For deployment, consider records outside the acting agent’s write permissions, tamper-evident logging and a named owner who can restrict actions or pause the workflow when monitoring fails. These are proposed design choices, not safeguards validated by the present experiment.


Randomized or less readily identifiable audits may reduce the usefulness of predicting scrutiny, but require their own coverage tests, privacy rules and retention limits. Australia’s agentic-AI guidance connects human accountability with documented agent actions and an orchestration layer. The practical question is who has the authority and evidence to respond to an outage or an unreliable account, rather than who merely receives the alert.


What experiment would distinguish the explanations?
Cross announced auditing with private checking and public disclosure as separate factors, keeping rules fixed. Add several announced audit probabilities and equivalent phrasings, then compare repeated decisions from the same verified state. Measure truthful claims, checkable communication and fulfilled commitments separately. This would test whether behavior tracks the audit cue, disclosure, or the information available for the task.





05 Methods & sources
Reproducible numerical analysis and inspectable source material.
Research materials
and limits of inference.
Behavioral analysis
The false-claim analysis uses all 90 main-study games from experiments 69–74, instrument 2.2. It is computed from supplied game-level metrics rather than an independent rerun of the statement scorer.
The checkability table and plot reproduce the supplied means for games reaching turn 80. This eligibility rule differs from the all-game false-claim analysis. Its effect on results should be investigated.
Uncertainty
Seed-matched intervals are exploratory percentile bootstrap intervals over 20 matched seed blocks. They are not the original primary tests. Repeated boards, sparse outcomes and small per-game denominators limit precision.
Generated accounts
The 96 notes were selected for references to auditing and grouped in an exploratory source review. Counts therefore describe this collection, rather than the prevalence of monitoring awareness across all decisions.
The selected episodes show original private notes, public lines and recorded resource holdings. All 96 full contexts and provenance records can be downloaded from their entries. These notes are generated outputs, not verified access to hidden cognition.
Scope of the evidence
The scorer classifications and exploratory note coding have not been independently recoded. Sparse false claims limit precision, and the surrogate’s modest held-out performance limits its explanatory reach. Controlled replays would be needed to isolate a cue’s influence on an individual reconstructed choice. Full technical provenance is documented in the repository.
